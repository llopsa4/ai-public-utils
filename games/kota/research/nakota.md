## Who "Nakota" means (with sources)

Two different usages exist. Every finding below is tagged **[A]** (Assiniboine / Nakoda / Stoney) or **[Y]** (Yankton-Yanktonai).

- **[A] Assiniboine call themselves Nakoda / Nakota.** Fort Belknap's own history page: "Assiniboine call themselves Nakoda or Nakota", glossed "the generous ones"; "Assiniboine" is Chippewa, "One who cooks with stones". https://ftbelknap.org/history
- **[A] Spelling varies by community.** Robert Four Star (chief of the Red Bottom Band, teaches Assiniboine at Fort Peck Community College) writes "Nakona / Nakonabi", "The Friendly People"; the editor notes "Nakoda" and "Nakodabi" are modern forms showing Sioux influence. https://mtprof.msun.edu/Win2003/r4star.html
- **[A] Stoney Nakoda Nations (Alberta)** call themselves "peoples of the mountains", *Iyarhe Nakoda*, formerly *Iyethkabi*; their language derives from the Nakoda dialect of the Sioux-Assiniboine family. https://stoneynakodanations.com/?p=223
- **[A] Saskatchewan entry (University of Saskatchewan, author David Reed Miller):** "Nakota is their name for themselves and the language that they speak"; this page does not use "Nakoda". https://teaching.usask.ca/indigenoussk/import/nakota_assiniboine.php
- **[A] Dialect framing:** the Alberta Heritage Community Foundation page names Nakoda as one of three Siouan dialects alongside Lakoda and Dakoda. https://wayback.archive-it.org/2217/20101208161855/http:/www.albertasource.ca/treaty7/traditional/nakoda.html (archived 2010, no named author)
- **[Y] Yankton as "Nakota-speaking".** The Lewis and Clark Trust describes the Yankton (Ihanktonwan, "end village") as a "Nakota-speaking" division of the Oceti Sakowin; the Yankton-affiliated Ihanktonwan Community College says it preserves "Nakota/Dakota" language. https://research.lewisandclarktrust.org/key-figure/yankton-sioux-tribe (museum/research, not nation-authored); college described in a travel/aggregator summary, not directly fetched.
- **[Y] The disagreement.** Linguists Parks and DeMallie (Sioux Dialect Survey, 1992, as relayed on the Siouan linguistics list) group Yankton-Yanktonai as one dialect distinct from Teton, Santee-Sisseton, Assiniboine and Stoney; the same thread says Yankton/Yanktonai were "erroneously classified as Nakota" by some. https://listserv.linguistlist.org/pipermail/siouan/2005-January/005303.html (academic, secondhand)
- **Relationship.** Assiniboine ancestors are reported to have split from the Yanktonai in the 17th century (summary only; sources differ on date and etymology; not verified from a nation source). Fort Belknap, however, gives an oral-history origin around "the Lake of the Woods and the Lake Winnipeg area". https://ftbelknap.org/history
- **Practical consequence:** the Assiniboine/Nakoda/Stoney reading has far more nation-authored material here. The Yankton reading has almost none fetched. Recommend the user choose one reading before design (see Open questions).

## Homeland and landscapes (for home-region rooms)

- **[A] Origin region (oral history):** "the Lake of the Woods and the Lake Winnipeg area of Canada"; bands later spread from White Earth, Minnesota, west to the Sweet Grass Hills of Montana. https://ftbelknap.org/history
- **[A] 17th century:** west from Lake Winnipeg and the Forks of the Red and Assiniboine rivers into central and southern Saskatchewan; mid-1800s from the Moose and Wood mountains to the Cypress Hills, North Saskatchewan River south to the Milk and Missouri; by 1840 about three-quarters lived along the Missouri in northwestern North Dakota and northeastern Montana. https://teaching.usask.ca/indigenoussk/import/nakota_assiniboine.php
- **[A] Oral tradition, Fort Peck:** northern Great Plains from Hudson's Bay and Lake Superior to the Rocky Mountains; southern bands wintered from the mouth of the Milk River to North Dakota. https://mtprof.msun.edu/Win2003/r4star.html
- **[A] Cypress Hills** are the homeland of Carry the Kettle ancestors (Wikipedia-level source only; verify on cegakin.ca). https://www.cegakin.ca/about
- **[A, Stoney] Mountains and foothills:** Great Plains to Rocky Mountain foothills and watersheds and over passes into the BC interior. Named places: *Minnewanka* "sacred waters"; *Kiska* "big horn sheep"; *Iya Mnathka* "flat faced mountain" (Yamnuska / Mount John Laurie). Places were often named for animals or events. https://stoneynakodanations.com/?p=223
- **[A, Stoney] Treaty 7 context:** they camped across the Oldman River from the Blackfoot and Tsuu T'ina at the 1877 talks. https://wayback.archive-it.org/2217/20101208161855/http:/www.albertasource.ca/treaty7/traditional/nakoda.html
- **[A, woodland/plains]** Woodland groups lived by lakes and rivers and used canoes; plains groups used buffalo pounds. https://teaching.usask.ca/indigenoussk/import/nakota_assiniboine.php
- **[Y] Homeland** centred on southwestern Minnesota pipestone country and the James and Missouri valleys of South Dakota. https://research.lewisandclarktrust.org/key-figure/yankton-sioux-tribe . Caution: the pipestone quarry is sacred (see Restricted); the landscape may be shown only as terrain, never the quarrying.

Room ideas that are source-supported: Cypress Hills, Missouri and Milk river bottoms, buffalo-pound bluff and trench, wooded lake shore (Lake Winnipeg region), foothills and mountain with sheep (Stoney).

## Publicly told stories with defeatable monsters (story, monster, how defeated, publicly told/published; ONLY candidates clearly NOT sacred beings honoured today)

Honest finding: no fully suitable candidate was verified. One partial candidate, plus one false lead corrected.

1. **The Crane and the Otter [A].** Monster: Osni' (Cold), who kills the Otter and enslaves the crane; "Thunder and lightning destroy him." Published by R. H. Lowie, *The Assiniboine*, via https://www.native-languages.org/assiniboinestory.htm . Issues: (a) academic collector, not nation-authored; (b) Thunder is probably a sacred being in Dakota-family traditions, so do not show Thunder as the defeater or as a power-up without user and nation guidance; (c) Cold is a weather force, not clearly a "monster" fought in melee. Doubtful; needs the user's decision and a nation-authored version.
2. **False lead, excluded.** The Sacred Texts "Swallowing Monster" (Spider rescues a girl) is a **Jicarilla Apache** myth, not Assiniboine; only its swing episode is cross-referenced to the Assiniboine in a footnote (Lowie p. 157). https://neonvagabond.xyz/mirrors/sacred-texts/nam/pla/jat/jat19.htm . Do not use it for Nakota.
3. **Not found.** No Stoney, Fort Peck, Fort Belknap or Yankton monster stories were retrieved. The Indiana "Rose Weasel" narratives from Fort Belknap reportedly include Inktomi tales (title seen: "Inktomi and the Ducks") but the file could not be opened and no monster plot was confirmed. https://aisri.sitehost.iu.edu/research/assiniboine/weasel-volume-english-complete.pdf (certificate error). ## Helper figures (animal relatives / elders who help in stories; flag any that are sacred)

- **Otter (in The Crane and the Otter) [A]:** keeps the young crane warm in a hole through winter and is rewarded with the ability to live in water without freezing. Source as above. Not flagged sacred in source. Source level: Lowie via native-languages.org.

## Clothing by gender (everyday, not ceremonial regalia)

All **[A]**, from the Heritage Community Foundation (Alberta Online Encyclopedia, Treaty 6; archived 2010; cites DeMallie and Miller 2001 and Denig 2000; not nation-authored): https://wayback.archive-it.org/2217/20101208163413/http:/www.albertasource.ca/treaty6/traditional_life/assiniboine_nakoda_nation/customs_and_traditions.html

- **Women:** "a dress made of bison cow hide bound at the waist by a leather thong"; leggings of elk hide.
- **Men:** shirts and leggings of antelope, deer or bison hide, used mainly in winter; breech cloths of blanket material or cloth.
- **Both:** "soft hide moccasins with rawhide soles", lined with bison hair in winter; winter capotes (hooded blanket coats) and fur caps.
- **Decoration** for special occasions or war (dyed porcupine quills, glass beads, paint, sometimes coastal shells) is ceremonial/occasional; keep out of everyday outfits. Human hair locks trimming garments: do not use (see Off-limits in CULTURE.md).
- Fort Belknap: buffalo hides were used for clothing and containers. https://ftbelknap.org/history
- **[Y] clothing:** not found. Do not mix with Assiniboine dress (CULTURE.md bars mixing nations' dress).

## Tools and weapons

- **Stone boiling [A]:** fist-size stones heated red hot and dropped into hide containers of water for soups and stews (the "cook with stones" name). https://mtprof.msun.edu/Win2003/r4star.html ; https://ftbelknap.org/history
- **Hide bags/containers, spits, pemmican, dog-drawn travois [A]:** meat roasted on spits or boiled in hide bags; pemmican for trade and use; dogs carried packs and pulled travois (the only domestic animal before horses). https://ftbelknap.org/history
- **Buffalo pound [A]:** structure used to trap and process bison. https://teaching.usask.ca/indigenoussk/import/nakota_assiniboine.php ; described as leading herds down a narrow trench to a bluff. Heritage Community Foundation page above.
- **Bows and arrows [A]:** used on horseback; guns "too cumbersome for use on horseback". Heritage Community Foundation page above.
- **Tipi [A]:** early form three poles lashed and spread into a tripod covered with hide; later larger tipis, set up and taken down by women. Heritage Community Foundation page above.
- **Canoes [A]:** woodland groups were skilled canoe users. https://teaching.usask.ca/indigenoussk/import/nakota_assiniboine.php
- **Metal tools and arms** came through European traders. Alberta Treaty 7 page above.
- Specific named tools (scrapers, clubs, shields): not found.

## Fighting traditions — factual

- **[A] Hunting methods that could inspire combat-free mechanics:** surround by mounted riders with bows and arrows; pound; solitary stalk. Heritage Community Foundation page above.
- **[A] Reputation:** Four Star calls the people a warrior society that never backed down; neighbours confirmed this. https://mtprof.msun.edu/Win2003/r4star.html . He also tells that warriors dared one another to wear smallpox victims' belongings: sensitive history, do not use as game content.
- **[A] Alliances and enemies:** allied with the Cree from the earliest records, later with the Saulteaux; Stoney: warfare with the Blackfoot and Tsuu T'ina "was common". https://teaching.usask.ca/indigenoussk/import/nakota_assiniboine.php ; Alberta Treaty 7 page above.
- **[A] Historical harm:** the 1873 Cypress Hills Massacre of ancestors of Carry the Kettle people (Wikipedia-level source; verify on the nation's site). Treat as history to honour, not a level.
- Ritual war practices, counting coup, war honours, society structures: not found, and in any case likely off-limits (trophies, regalia).

## Restricted or sacred — do NOT use in game (list with reason and source)

- **Vision seeking, sweat purification, sacred songs, prayer:** Fort Belknap names religion as centred on visions, prayer, sacred songs and sweat purification before major ceremonies; sweetgrass as incense. https://ftbelknap.org/history . Reason: matches CULTURE.md off-limits (no ceremonies, no sacred practice as mechanics); sweetgrass burning must not be a buff or item.
- **Sacred ceremonies generally:** Stoney Nakoda say they "continue to practice their ceremonies, culture, and values". https://stoneynakodanations.com/?p=223 . Reason: living practice.
- **Thunder and similar beings:** the Cold story ends with Thunder and lightning destroying the antagonist. Reason: probable sacred beings; flagged by the agent as probable, **not source-confirmed**; user to rule. Never make them foes; do not use as a power.
- **Pipestone quarry (Yankton homeland) and the pipe:** the quarry is tied to the pipe; sacred object; not found in a nation source here. Show at most the landscape if the user approves.
- **Human hair locks and war decoration; smallpox-victim belongings:** trophies / sensitive history. Heritage Community Foundation page above; Four Star above.
- **Medicine keepers:** Four Star notes the Red Bottom name may derive from the band's reputation as keepers of medicines. https://mtprof.msun.edu/Win2003/r4star.html . Reason: do not turn medicines into items or mystical-shaman stereotypes.
