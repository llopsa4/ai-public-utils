# Kota cultural authority

Kota depicts a real, living culture. This file is **reference, not binding** (user, 2026-11-18):
game content may be invented when it is plainly fantasy (weapons, items, spirit enemies, arenas,
props, upgrades). Claims about the real culture (named beings, history, words, what Dakota people
did) still need a source here; gaps go to *Needs review*. Claude's own limits always apply (see
*Off-limits*). Only the user adds or changes entries.

Trusted sources, with what each one may be used for.

corroborate, never be the sole source. Individual sources are listed here once the user approves them.

## May show

What the game may depict, and how.

- **Iya, the Camp-Eater (Dakota telling only)**, approved by the user 2026-11-18. Use: the Dakota
  boss. A monster that comes disguised as what people most want and swallows a camp; when he is
  defeated, the camp walks out freed (*released, not taken*). Sources: Zitkala-Ša, *Old Indian
  Legends* (1901); Deloria, *Dakota Texts* (1932, title only); a Dakota storyteller's public
  telling (Seattle Channel, 2020). Details: [research/dakota.md](research/dakota.md). **Community
  check done by the user, 2026-11-18** (they talked it through with a local community member); no
  further check needed before publishing.
- **Approved with the slice spec, 2026-11-18** (user; details in [SLICE.md](SLICE.md), sources in
  [research/dakota.md](research/dakota.md)):
  - **Gift helpers** from Zitkala-Ša: Skiska the duck, Patkasa the turtle, the muskrat, the
    coyote, Manstin the rabbit, the youngest bear.
  - **Dakota home region:** the Minnesota River Valley (Mdewakanton homeland): woods, lakes, rivers,
    maple-sugar groves, a wild-rice lake, prairie with sage and willow. Bdote and Bde Wakan are never
    arenas.
  - **Woman hero's dress:** a buckskin dress with short sleeves, beads and porcupine quills, and
    moccasins (Eastman). **Buffalo robe** (hair inside, wide belt) is allowed as an equippable garment.
  - **Tools:** stone axe, short-handled axe, bow (no poisoned arrows), knife in a beaded case.
  - **Food:** wild rice, maple sugar and dried meat (*land renews* pickups). Archery games and
    lacrosse may appear on the camp practice ground.
  - **Place names:** *Mni Sota Makoce* and *Cansa'yapi*, spelled as on the Lower Sioux site.
  - Invented, shapeless spirit enemies (frost wisp, hunger shade, blight swarm) make no cultural
    claim.

## Off-limits

What the game must not depict or imitate (for example sacred ceremonies or objects), and why.
Set by the user, 2026-11-17; sources may add more.

**Reference, not binding since 2026-11-18** (user: invention is allowed). Claude's own limits still
apply to every design, text and art piece.

- **No ceremonies as mechanics.** Sacred ceremonies and sacred objects (for example the pipe, sweat
  lodge, vision seeking, Sun Dance) are never buffs, items, minigames or animations; at most they are
  mentioned respectfully in stories. *Why:* they are living sacred practice, not game content.
- **No sacred beings as foes.** Beings honoured as sacred are never enemies or bosses; only monsters
  the stories themselves show being defeated may be fought.
- **No trophies or regalia tiers.** No scalping or trophies from bodies; eagle feathers and
  headdresses are never loot, rarity tiers or cosmetic unlocks. Nothing is taken from the fallen.
- **No real people or stereotypes.** No real historical individuals as characters; no "savage",
  mystical-shaman or alcohol stereotypes, no war-whoop sounds, no mixing of one nation's dress with
  another's.
- **Lakota Iya** is never a foe: some Lakota accounts treat Iya as a sacred being (user, 2026-11-18).

## Language and naming

Rules for Lakota, Dakota and Nakota words, names, spelling and translation in the game.

- Game text is English. A native word is used only when an approved source here gives its spelling,
  meaning and nation/dialect; its translation is always available to the player.
- No invented words, and no native-language voice unless a speaker records it.
